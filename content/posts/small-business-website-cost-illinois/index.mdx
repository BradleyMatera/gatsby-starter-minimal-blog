## The actual cost breakdown

A small business website has three cost layers: the build, the ongoing hosting and care, and the extras. Here is what each one actually costs.

### Domain

A domain name (yourbusiness.com) runs about $15 per year. Some registrars charge $10 for the first year and $20 after that. I do not mark up domains. You register it in your own name and you own it. If someone else registered your domain for you and holds it in their account, that is a problem. I cover that in my post on [website ownership](/website-ownership-domain-hosting-content).

### Hosting and ongoing care

Hosting alone costs $5 to $15 per month on a shared host. But hosting alone does not keep your site secure, updated, or backed up. My care plans bundle hosting, SSL, security monitoring, backups, and small content updates:

- **Starter care:** $37/month
- **Growth care:** $67/month
- **Premium care:** $97/month

The difference is how many content updates are included and how many pages the site has. You are not locked in. You can cancel anytime and I will help you migrate.

### The build

This is the one-time cost to design and build the site. My package pricing:

| Package | Build Cost | Pages | What is included |
|---------|-----------|-------|------------------|
| Starter | $447 | Up to 5 | Custom design, mobile responsive, contact form, basic SEO setup |
| Growth | $797 | Up to 8 | Everything in Starter plus blog setup, Google Analytics, schema markup |
| Premium | $1,497 | Up to 15 | Everything in Growth plus custom features, lead capture, local SEO landing pages |

A Site Refresh is $597 if you already have a site and just need it rebuilt or updated.

### What costs extra

- **Copywriting:** If you do not have text for your pages, I charge $65/hour or we scope it as a separate project. Most businesses write their own copy and I edit it.
- **Stock photography:** I use free licensed images by default. Custom photography or paid stock is billed at cost.
- **Logo design:** I can do a simple text-based logo. A full brand identity is a separate project.
- **E-commerce:** A full store with payment processing is not included in the packages above. That is scoped separately.
- **Ongoing marketing:** SEO campaigns, Google Ads management, and content marketing are separate from care plans.

## How this compares to agency pricing

I am not going to tell you agencies are bad. They are not. They serve a real market. But you should know what you are paying for when you hire one.

A typical agency in Illinois charges $3,000 to $8,000 for a 5 to 10 page small business website. Some charge more. Here is where that money goes:

| Cost category | Agency | Solo developer (me) |
|--------------|--------|---------------------|
| Project manager | $500-1,000 | $0 (you talk to me directly) |
| Designer salary | $800-1,500 | Included in build price |
| Developer salary | $800-1,500 | Included in build price |
| Agency overhead (office, software, sales) | $500-1,500 | $0 |
| Account management and meetings | $400-800 | $0 |
| **Total** | **$3,000-8,000** | **$447-1,497** |

The agency is not overcharging for what they deliver. They have payroll, office costs, software licenses, and a sales process. You are paying for that infrastructure. If you need a team of people, a complex project, or ongoing marketing support, an agency makes sense. I talk about when that is the right call in my post on [freelancer vs agency](/freelancer-vs-agency-local-business).

If you need a clean, fast, professional website and you want to talk to the person building it, a solo developer is the cheaper path.

## Why a solo developer is cheaper

The math is simple. I do not have a project manager, a sales team, an office lease, or a marketing budget. I do not have a Monday standup meeting about your project. I just build it. The cost savings from not carrying that overhead get passed to you.

That does not mean you get less. You get the same technical output: a responsive site, clean code, proper SEO setup, fast load times, and a contact form that works. What you do not get is the agency process around it. For most small businesses, the process is not what they need. They need a website that works.

## Is the cheapest option always right?

No. If you are running an online store, need a custom booking system, or want a full marketing team behind your site, the cheapest build is not the right fit. That is why I scope every project before quoting. Sometimes I tell people to go elsewhere because what they need is bigger than what I do.

But if you are a plumber in Rockford, a salon in Springfield, or a consultant in Chicago who needs a professional site that shows up in local search and converts visitors into calls, the pricing above is what you should expect to pay.
