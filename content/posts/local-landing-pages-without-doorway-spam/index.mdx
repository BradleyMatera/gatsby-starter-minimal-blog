City landing pages are one of the most effective local SEO tactics. They are also one of the most abused. For every business with a useful, honest city page, there are ten with thin templates that swap the city name and call it a day. Google calls those doorway pages, and they do not rank anymore. This guide explains the difference and how to build pages that actually work.

## The short answer

A good local landing page has unique content about your actual work in that city: real examples, city-specific FAQs, genuine local context, and a clear service radius. A doorway page is a template with the city name swapped in, no unique information, and no real connection to the area. Google's guidelines explicitly prohibit doorway pages. Build one page per city you actually serve, write real content for each, and link them together honestly.

## What is a doorway page?

Google defines doorway pages as pages created to rank for specific search queries but that lead users to the same destination. In local SEO, this usually looks like:

- A single template with `{City}` as a variable
- 50 pages that are 90% identical
- No unique content beyond the city name and a few swapped keywords
- All pages linking to the same contact form or phone number
- No real reason for the page to exist other than ranking

Example of a doorway page:

> "Looking for a plumber in `{City}`? We provide plumbing services in `{City}` and the surrounding area. Our `{City}` plumbers are licensed and insured. Call us for all your `{City}` plumbing needs."

That paragraph with "Rockford" swapped for "Belvidere" swapped for "Machesney Park" is a doorway page. Google has been penalizing these since 2015.

## Google's guidelines on doorway pages

From Google's Search Central documentation:

> "Doorway pages are pages that are created primarily for search engines, not for human users. These pages are built to rank for specific search queries and typically lead users to the same destination."

Google specifically calls out local doorway pages as a violation:

> "Having multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page"

If you have 20 city pages that all link to the same contact page with no other unique content, you are funneling users. That is a doorway page.

## What makes a local landing page good

A good city page exists because you actually serve that city and have something real to say about it. It is not a template. It is a page a human would find useful.

### Real local context

Mention specific neighborhoods, landmarks, or streets. If you are a plumber writing about Rockford, you might reference:

- The older housing stock in the near-east side that needs repiping
- The new developments on the east side with different plumbing setups
- Specific neighborhoods you have worked in: Edgebrook, Churchill Grove, Haight Village

This proves you actually work there. A doorway page cannot do this because it has no real knowledge of the city.

### Real examples and case studies

Include 2-3 short examples of jobs you have done in that city. Not full case studies, just a paragraph each:

> "Replaced a 50-gallon water heater for a homeowner in the Churchill Grove neighborhood. The old unit was 14 years old and leaking from the bottom. New unit installed in 4 hours, same day."

This is content a template cannot generate. It is specific, honest, and useful.

### City-specific FAQs

Every city has different questions. Some are universal ("Do you offer free estimates?") but some are local:

- "Do you charge a travel fee to come to Belvidere?"
- "How long does it take you to get to Machesney Park during rush hour?"
- "Are you familiar with the older homes in the Haight Village historic district?"

Write 5-8 FAQs per city page. Make them real questions you have been asked, not invented ones.

### Actual service radius

Be honest about where you will and will not go. If you serve Rockford but not Freeport, say so. If you charge a travel fee beyond 20 miles, mention it. This builds trust and filters out bad leads.

Include a map or a list of specific ZIP codes you cover. This is more useful than "serving the surrounding area."

### Unique title and meta description

Each page needs its own title tag and meta description. Not "Plumber in `{City}` | Your Company" on every page. Write something specific:

- "Plumber in Rockford, IL | Water Heater Repair and Repipes"
- "Plumber in Belvidere, IL | Drain Cleaning and Emergency Service"

The meta description should mention the city and your specific services.

## What to avoid

### City name swap templates

If you can generate the page by running a find-and-replace on the city name, it is a doorway page. Do not do this. Even if it ranks for a few months, Google will catch up.

### Fake addresses on city pages

Some businesses put a fake address on each city page to make it look like they have an office there. This is a fast track to a GBP suspension and a manual penalty on your website. Do not do it.

### Thin content pages

If your city page has 150 words and two of them are the city name, it is thin. Aim for 500-800 words of genuinely useful content per page. If you cannot write 500 words about your work in a city, you probably do not serve that city well enough to justify a page.

### Duplicate content across pages

Even if you are not doing a full template swap, be careful about reusing paragraphs. If your "about us" section is identical on every city page, Google sees that as duplicate content. Write a unique intro for each.

## How to structure your city pages

### URL structure

Use clean, descriptive URLs:

- `/service-areas/rockford/`
- `/service-areas/belvidere/`
- `/service-areas/loves-park/`

Do not use parameters or subdomains. Keep it simple.

### Internal linking

Link your city pages together. Each page should link to 2-3 nearby city pages and to your main service pages. This helps Google understand the relationship between your pages and distributes link equity.

Add a service areas index page that lists all your city pages with a short description of each. You can see how I do this on my [service areas](/service-areas/) page.

### Schema markup

Add `Service` or `ProfessionalService` schema to each city page with the city in the `areaServed` field. This tells Google explicitly which areas each page covers.

## How many city pages should you create?

One per city you actually serve. Not one per city you wish you served. If you regularly do jobs in 4 cities, make 4 pages. If you serve 12, make 12. Quality matters more than quantity. 4 excellent city pages will outperform 20 thin ones every time.
