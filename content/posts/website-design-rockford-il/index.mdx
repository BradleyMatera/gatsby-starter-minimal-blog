## Why most Rockford web design companies do not post prices

There are two reasons most local web design companies will not put prices on their website.

The first is legitimate: every project is different, and a plumber needs something different than a law firm. A fixed price list can feel misleading.

The second is not legitimate: they want to get you on a sales call so they can upsell you. If you do not know the going rate, you have no leverage. The longer they can keep you in the dark, the more they can charge.

A web designer who posts prices is telling you they respect your time. A web designer who hides prices behind a "get a quote" form is telling you their sales process matters more than your budget.

This is not a minor red flag. It is the biggest one.

## Agency vs. solo developer for a Rockford small business site

Most Rockford small businesses — plumbers, HVAC, salons, restaurants, consultants, attorneys — need a 5 to 10 page site that loads fast, shows up in local search, and converts visitors into calls. You do not need a 12-person team for that.

You need an agency if:

- You are building a custom web application, not a marketing site
- You need ongoing marketing, paid ads, and content production under one roof
- You have an internal team that needs a vendor with account management
- Your project has multiple stakeholders and needs a formal process

You need a solo developer if:

- You want to talk to the person actually building your site
- You want a fast, clean, accessible site without paying for overhead
- You have a defined scope and want it done, not managed

## What to look for in a Rockford web designer

### WCAG accessibility compliance

This is non-negotiable in 2026. The ADA applies to websites, and plaintiffs' firms have been sending demand letters to small businesses across Illinois for years. A site that is not WCAG 2.1 AA compliant is a liability, not just a usability issue.

Ask any designer: "Will my site meet WCAG 2.1 AA?" If they cannot answer that question specifically, do not hire them. "Accessible" is not a answer. "WCAG 2.1 AA, tested with axe and Lighthouse, with manual keyboard navigation checks" is an answer.

I wrote a full breakdown in [website accessibility basics](/website-accessibility-basics).

### Mobile speed

More than 60% of local searches happen on a phone. If your site takes 4 seconds to load on mobile, half your visitors are gone before they see your phone number.

Google's Core Web Vitals are a ranking factor. A site that scores well on Lighthouse (90+ performance) will rank better and convert better. Ask any designer what their sites score on mobile Lighthouse. If they do not know, they are not measuring it.

### Local SEO setup

A website is not useful if it does not show up when someone searches "your service Rockford IL." Your site needs:

- Title tags and meta descriptions with your service and city
- Schema markup (LocalBusiness, Service, FAQPage)
- A Google Business Profile linked from the site
- NAP consistency (name, address, phone) across the site and all directories
- Local landing pages if you serve multiple cities

This should be included in the build, not sold as a separate $1,500 "SEO package."

## Questions to ask any Rockford web designer before hiring

Print this list. Ask every one of these on the first call.

1. **What is the total cost, including everything?** Not "starting at." The actual number.
2. **What is included in the monthly fee, and what costs extra?**
3. **Will the site meet WCAG 2.1 AA?** Get it in writing.
4. **What will the mobile Lighthouse score be?** Anything under 80 is a problem.
5. **Do you set up Google Business Profile and schema markup, or is that extra?**
6. **Who owns the domain and hosting account?** It should be you, not them.
7. **What happens if I want to leave?** Can you take your site with you?
8. **Can I see three live sites you built, and run Lighthouse on them myself?**
9. **What is your timeline, and what delays it?**
10. **Do you have an accessibility statement on your own site?**

If a designer cannot answer these without hedging, keep looking.

ADA website lawsuits are not theoretical. Thousands of small businesses get demand letters every year, and Illinois is one of the more active states for these filings. The cost of defending one — even if you win — is $5,000 to $25,000 in legal fees. The cost of building an accessible site from the start is $0 extra if your designer knows what they are doing.

## The problem with "custom programming" pitches

Some Rockford web design companies pitch "custom programming" as a premium feature. Here is the reality: a 5-page small business website does not need custom programming. It needs clean, semantic HTML, modern CSS, and maybe a contact form handler. That is not custom programming. That is just building a website.

You need custom programming if:

- You are building a booking system with real-time availability
- You need a customer portal with authentication
- You are integrating with a proprietary CRM or ERP
- You need a custom e-commerce workflow

You do not need custom programming if:

- You need a contact form (that is a standard form handler)
- You need a map (that is an embed)
- You need a photo gallery (that is standard layout)
- You need a blog (that is a CMS)

If a designer quotes you $3,000 for "custom programming" on a contact form and a photo gallery, that is an upsell, not a feature.

## How to check if a web designer's own site is any good

A web designer's own website is their portfolio. If it is slow, inaccessible, or broken on mobile, that is what you are going to get.

Run this 60-second audit on any Rockford web designer's site before you contact them:

1. Open their site on your phone. Does it load in under 3 seconds?
2. Run [Lighthouse](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) on their homepage. Are performance, accessibility, and SEO all 90+?
3. Try to navigate their site with only your keyboard (Tab key). Can you reach every link and button?
4. Check if they have an accessibility statement. If they do not, they are not taking it seriously.
5. Look at their blog. Is the most recent post within the last 3 months? A dead blog means a dead business.

If a designer's own site fails these checks, they cannot build a site that passes them for you.

## Timeline expectations

A competent solo developer can build a 5-page small business site in about 14 days, assuming you provide content on time. A larger 10 to 15 page site takes 3 to 4 weeks. Anything longer than that is usually caused by the client not providing content, not by the build itself.

If a designer tells you a 5-page site takes 8 weeks, ask why. Either they are juggling too many projects, or they are padding the timeline to justify a higher price.

| Project size | Realistic timeline |
|--------------|-------------------|
| 5-page site | 10-14 days |
| 8-page site with blog | 2-3 weeks |
| 15-page site with local landing pages | 3-4 weeks |
| Site refresh | 7-10 days |

The biggest delay is always content. If you have your text, photos, and logo ready before the project starts, you will cut the timeline in half.

## Red flags when hiring a Rockford web designer

Walk away if you see any of these:

- **No pricing anywhere.** They want you on a call before they tell you what it costs.
- **No portfolio.** If they cannot show you live sites they built, they have not built any.
- **No accessibility statement.** They are not thinking about ADA risk, which means they are passing that risk to you.
- **A dead blog.** The last post was in 2022. The business is on autopilot.
- **"Custom programming" for a basic site.** It is an upsell.
- **They own your domain.** Your domain should be in your name, in your account. If they registered it for you and hold it, you do not own your website.
- **A monthly retainer with no deliverables.** If they cannot tell you what they will do each month, in writing, you are paying for nothing.
