NAP consistency is boring. It is also one of the few local SEO tasks that you can fully control, and getting it wrong quietly drags down your rankings for months before you notice. This guide explains what NAP is, why inconsistency hurts, and how to audit and fix your listings without paying a service $300 a year.

## The short answer

NAP stands for Name, Address, and Phone number. It must be identical across every website that lists your business, including Google, Bing, Apple Maps, Yelp, Facebook, and the Better Business Bureau. Inconsistent NAP confuses Google about which listing is correct, which lowers your trust score and drops your local rankings. Audit your listings twice a year, fix any mismatches, and never change your business name, address, or phone without updating every directory.

## What is NAP?

NAP is the three pieces of information that identify your business across the web:

- **Name:** Your business name, exactly as registered
- **Address:** Your physical location, formatted consistently
- **Phone:** Your primary phone number, in the same format everywhere

Some directories ask for more (website, hours, categories), but NAP is the core. Google crawls the web looking for your NAP on other sites. Each consistent mention is a vote of confidence. Each inconsistent mention is a contradiction.

## Why inconsistent NAP hurts rankings

Google's local algorithm uses citations as a trust signal. If 30 websites say your business is at "123 Main St, Rockford, IL 61101" with phone "(815) 555-1234," Google is confident that is correct. If 5 of those sites say "123 Main Street" and 3 say "(815) 555-1234 ext 2," Google is less confident.

When Google is unsure, it ranks you lower. Not because you are being penalized, but because it would rather show a business it is confident about than one it isn't.

### Common inconsistency sources

1. **You moved** and updated Google but not Yelp, Facebook, or the BBB
2. **You changed phone numbers** when switching to VoIP or Google Voice
3. **An old employee** created listings with a personal phone number
4. **Data aggregators** scraped an old version of your info and spread it
5. **You used different abbreviations** ("St" vs "Street", "IL" vs "Illinois")

The abbreviation issue is real. Google does not care about "St" vs "Street" on its own, but if half your citations say one and half say the other, it looks inconsistent. Pick one format and use it everywhere.

## How to audit your citations

### Step 1: Write down your canonical NAP

Decide on your official format and write it down. Example:

```
Matera Plumbing
456 Industrial Ave
Rockford, IL 61109
(815) 555-1234
```

Rules:
- Use the exact legal name or DBA, no keywords added
- Use the USPS-standard address format
- Pick one phone number and stick with it
- Do not use tracking numbers in citations (see below)

### Step 2: Search for your business

Search Google for your business name, your phone number, and your old phone numbers. Search in quotes:

- `"Matera Plumbing"`
- `"(815) 555-1234"`
- `"815-555-1234"`
- `"Matera Plumbing" Rockford`

This will surface most of your existing citations. You can also use free tools like the BrightLocal citation checker, but manual searching catches more.

### Step 3: Check the major directories

Go through each of these and verify your NAP matches:

| Directory | URL | Notes |
|-----------|-----|-------|
| Google Business Profile | business.google.com | Most important |
| Bing Places | bingplaces.com | Second most important |
| Apple Maps | business.apple.com | Growing fast |
| Yelp | biz.yelp.com | Hard to fix, do it first |
| Facebook | facebook.com/business | Check your page info |
| BBB | bbb.org | If you are accredited |
| Yellow Pages | yellowpages.com | Still sends data to others |
| Foursquare | foursquare.com | Feeds Apple Maps |
| MapQuest | mapquest.com | Lower priority but free |
| TripAdvisor | tripadvisor.com | Only if relevant to your industry |

### Step 4: Document the mismatches

Create a spreadsheet. Columns: Directory, Current Name, Current Address, Current Phone, Correct Name, Correct Address, Correct Phone, Status. Fill it in as you audit.

You will be surprised how many are wrong. Most businesses I audit have 5-10 inconsistencies across the top directories.

### Step 5: Fix them

Go to each directory, claim the listing if you haven't, and update the NAP to match your canonical format. Some directories make this easy. Yelp and Yellow Pages can be stubborn. Be persistent.

## Tools to manage citations

If you have more than 20 citations to manage, manual updates get tedious. These tools help:

| Tool | Cost | What it does |
|------|------|-------------|
| BrightLocal | $39/mo | Audit, build, and fix citations |
| Whitespark | $27/mo | Citation building and cleanup |
| Yext | $99/mo | Power listings, stops syncing when you cancel |
| Semrush Listing Management | Included in Semrush | Syncs to major directories |

I do not recommend Yext for most small businesses. When you cancel, your listings revert to whatever the data aggregators had before. You are renting your citations, not owning them. BrightLocal and Whitespark make permanent changes.

For a business with 5-10 citations, do it manually. It takes an afternoon and you own the results.

## Common mistakes

### Using a virtual office address

This is the most damaging mistake. You rent a virtual office in a better part of town, use that address on your citations, and think you are tricking Google. You are not. Google cross-references addresses with street view, other listings, and business registration data. If it detects a virtual office, it suspends your listing.

Use your real address. If you are a service-area business, hide it on Google but use it consistently on directories that require it.

### Different phone numbers on different listings

Some businesses use a tracking number on Yelp, a different one on their website, and their real number on Google. This is a mess. Google sees three different phone numbers and does not know which is real.

Use one phone number everywhere. If you need call tracking, use a service that forwards to your real number and uses call tracking numbers only in specific ad campaigns, not in organic citations.

### Not updating after a move

You moved 2 years ago. You updated Google. You forgot Yelp, Bing, and the BBB. Now Google sees conflicting addresses and your trust score drops.

Any time your address or phone changes, update every directory. Use your audit spreadsheet to make sure you don't miss any.

## How often to audit

Twice a year is enough for most businesses. Once a quarter if you are in a competitive market. The audit takes 1-2 hours if you stay on top of it, or a full day if you let it slide for years.
