## Why DeKalb businesses need a website built for two audiences

A DeKalb business serves two distinct populations. The first is the year-round resident — the family in a north-side subdivision, the farmer outside town, the professional commuting to the Chicago suburbs via I-88. The second is the NIU student, who is here for four years, lives on or near campus, and finds everything through their phone.

These two groups search differently. Residents search "DeKalb IL dentist" or "DeKalb County roofing." Students search "food near NIU" or "haircut DeKalb" and they expect the answer in three seconds on a 5G connection. If your site loads slowly, is hard to read on a phone, or does not show up in the map pack, the student is already at your competitor.

A website built for DeKalb has to win both searches. That means fast mobile load times, clean local SEO signals, and content that speaks to both audiences without alienating either one.

## The problem with companies that do not post prices

Most web design companies serving DeKalb will not put prices on their website. There are two reasons.

The first is legitimate: every project is different, and a restaurant near campus needs something different than a law firm on Lincoln Highway. A fixed price list can feel misleading.

The second is not legitimate: they want you on a sales call so they can upsell you. If you do not know the going rate, you have no leverage. The longer they keep you in the dark, the more they can charge.

A web designer who posts prices is telling you they respect your time. A web designer who hides prices behind a "get a quote" form is telling you their sales process matters more than your budget. That is the biggest red flag in this industry.

## Why accessibility matters for DeKalb businesses

ADA website lawsuits are not theoretical. Plaintiffs' firms have been sending demand letters to small businesses across Illinois for years, and DeKalb County is not exempt. The cost of defending one — even if you win — is $5,000 to $25,000 in legal fees. The cost of building an accessible site from the start is $0 extra if your designer knows what they are doing.

Ask any designer: "Will my site meet WCAG 2.1 AA?" If they cannot answer that question specifically, do not hire them. "Accessible" is not an answer. "WCAG 2.1 AA, tested with axe and Lighthouse, with manual keyboard navigation checks" is an answer.

## Mobile speed: most DeKalb business websites load too slowly

More than 60% of local searches happen on a phone, and in a college town that number is even higher — NIU students are not opening laptops to find a restaurant. If your site takes 4 seconds to load on mobile, half your visitors are gone before they see your phone number. I have run Lighthouse on a lot of DeKalb business websites. Most score between 30 and 60 on mobile performance. That is not good enough.

Google's Core Web Vitals are a ranking factor. A site that scores 90+ on mobile Lighthouse will rank better and convert better. Ask any designer what their sites score on mobile Lighthouse. If they do not know, they are not measuring it — and if they are not measuring it, they cannot improve it.

## Local SEO basics for DeKalb

A website is not useful if it does not show up when someone searches "your service DeKalb IL." Your site needs:

- **Google Business Profile.** Claim it, verify it, fill out every field, add photos, and get reviews. This is the single biggest local SEO lever for a DeKalb business.
- **Local citations.** Your name, address, and phone (NAP) must be identical across your site, Google Business Profile, Yelp, Bing Places, Apple Maps, and industry directories. I cover this in my post on [local citations and NAP consistency](/local-citations-nap-consistency).
- **Service-area pages.** If you serve DeKalb, Sycamore, and the surrounding county, have a page for each. Not duplicate pages with the city name swapped — real, useful content about serving that area. I wrote a guide on [local landing pages without doorway spam](/local-landing-pages-without-doorway-spam).
- **Schema markup.** LocalBusiness, Service, and FAQPage schema help Google understand what you do and where.
- **Title tags and meta descriptions** with your service and city.

This should be included in the build, not sold as a separate $1,500 "SEO package."

## Questions to ask before hiring anyone for a DeKalb website project

Print this list. Ask every one of these on the first call.

1. **What is the total cost, including everything?** Not "starting at." The actual number.
2. **What is included in the monthly fee, and what costs extra?**
3. **Will the site meet WCAG 2.1 AA?** Get it in writing.
4. **What will the mobile Lighthouse score be?** Anything under 80 is a problem.
5. **Do you set up Google Business Profile and schema markup, or is that extra?**
6. **Who owns the domain and hosting account?** It should be you, not them.
7. **What happens if I want to leave?** Can you take your site with you?
8. **Can I see three live sites you built, and run Lighthouse on them myself?**
9. **What is your timeline, and what delays it?**
10. **Do you have an accessibility statement on your own site?**

If a designer cannot answer these without hedging, keep looking.
