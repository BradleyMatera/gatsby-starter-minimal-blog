A service-area business is one where you go to the customer instead of the customer coming to you. Plumbers, electricians, landscapers, mobile detailers, HVAC companies, roofers. You don't have a showroom. You have a truck. Google has specific rules for how these businesses show up in local search, and if you break them, your listing gets suspended.

## The short answer

Set your Google Business Profile to a service-area business, hide your home address, list the cities you actually serve, build a unique landing page for each city, and get citations on the major directories with a consistent NAP. Do not use a fake address, a virtual office, or a friend's storefront. Google catches these and suspends the listing, sometimes permanently.

## What counts as a service-area business

Google defines a service-area business (SAB) as one that visits customers at their location but does not serve customers at its own address. If customers come to you, you are a storefront business. If you go to them, you are a service-area business.

Examples:
- A plumber who works out of their garage but visits customers' homes
- A mobile dog groomer who drives to clients
- An HVAC company with a warehouse but no walk-in counter

If you have a warehouse or shop but no customer-facing entrance, you are still an SAB. Google cares whether customers visit your location, not whether you have one.

## Google Business Profile settings for SABs

When you create or edit your GBP, there is a setting for service area. Here is how to configure it correctly.

### Hide your address

If you are an SAB, you must not show your address on your listing. Google's guidelines are explicit: service-area businesses should not list their address publicly.

In GBP manager:
1. Go to **Location details**
2. Under **Address**, enter your real address (Google needs it for verification)
3. Check **I deliver goods and services to my customers at their locations**
4. Under **Service area**, add the cities, regions, or ZIP codes you serve
5. Make sure **Show business address to customers** is not enabled

Your address stays on file with Google but does not appear in Maps or Search.

### List your service areas

You can add up to 20 service areas. Use cities or ZIP codes, not radius circles. Google deprecated radius-based service areas in 2021.

Be honest. List the places you actually go. If you are based in Rockford and you serve Loves Park, Machesney Park, and Belvidere, list those. Do not list Chicago because you did one job there in 2019.

Google uses your service area list to determine which local searches you are eligible for. If someone searches "plumber near me" from Belvidere and you listed Belvidere, you can show up. If you didn't list it, you won't.

### Choose the right category

Same rule as any business: pick the most specific accurate category. "Plumber" not "Services." The category determines eligibility, not the service area list.

## Landing page strategy for each city

Your GBP gets you into the map pack. Your website gets you into the organic results. For service-area businesses, the most effective strategy is a dedicated landing page for each city you serve.

### Why you need city pages

If you serve 5 cities and you have one services page, you are competing for all 5 cities on one page. Google has to figure out which city to rank you for, and it usually picks your headquarters city.

If you have 5 separate pages, each optimized for one city, each page can rank independently. Your Rockford page ranks for "plumber Rockford." Your Belvidere page ranks for "plumber Belvidere."

### What makes a good city page

Not a template with the city name swapped. Google calls those doorway pages and penalizes them. See my guide on [local landing pages without doorway spam](/local-landing-pages-without-doorway-spam/) for the full breakdown.

A good city page has:
- The city name in the title, H1, and URL
- Real content about your work in that city (specific jobs, neighborhoods, local references)
- A unique FAQ section answering questions relevant to that area
- Your service area map or list
- Reviews from customers in that city
- Internal links to your main service pages

### How many pages to create

One per city you actually serve. If you serve 3 cities, make 3 pages. If you serve 15, make 15. Do not create pages for cities you don't serve. That is spam, and it does not work long-term.

## Citation building for SABs

Citations are mentions of your business name, address, and phone number on other websites. For SABs, citations are especially important because you don't have a storefront to anchor you to a location.

### Where to get citations

| Directory | Cost | Priority |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Google Business Profile | Free | Must have |
| Bing Places | Free | Must have |
| Apple Maps | Free | High |
| Yelp | Free | High |
| Facebook | Free | High |
| Better Business Bureau | Paid | Medium |
| Yellow Pages | Paid | Low |
| Industry-specific directories | Varies | Medium |

### NAP consistency

Your Name, Address, and Phone number must be identical across every listing. Same formatting, same abbreviations, same phone number. If your GBP says "Matera Plumbing" and Yelp says "Matera Plumbing LLC," Google sees two different businesses.

For SABs, most directories will still ask for an address. Use your real address even though it is hidden on GBP. The directories need it for verification. Just make sure it matches everywhere.

## Why fake addresses get you suspended

This is the most common SAB mistake. You don't want to use your home address, so you use a coworking space, a virtual office, a friend's shop, or a PO Box.

Google's policy is clear: the address must be where you actually operate. If Google figures out you are using a virtual office, they suspend the listing. And they are getting better at detecting this. They cross-reference with street view, utility records, and other listings at the same address.

### What happens when you get suspended

- Your listing disappears from Maps and Search
- You lose all your reviews (sometimes permanently)
- You have to re-verify, which can take weeks
- Repeat violations can result in a permanent ban

It is not worth it. Use your real address, hide it, and let your service area settings do the work.

## The honest path

Service-area business SEO is slower than storefront SEO because you don't have the address signal. But it works. The businesses that rank are the ones that:

1. Set up GBP correctly as an SAB
2. Build real city landing pages
3. Maintain consistent citations
4. Collect reviews from customers in each service area
5. Post updates and answer Q&A
