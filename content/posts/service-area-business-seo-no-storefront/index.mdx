A service-area business is one where you go to the customer instead of the customer coming to you. Plumbers, electricians, landscapers, mobile detailers, HVAC companies, roofers. You don't have a showroom. You have a truck. Google has [specific rules](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038163) for how these businesses show up in local search, and if you break them, your listing gets suspended.

## The short answer

Set your Google Business Profile to a service-area business, hide your home address, list the cities you actually serve, build a unique landing page for each city, and get citations on the major directories with a consistent NAP. Do not use a fake address, a virtual office, or a friend's storefront. Google catches these and suspends the listing, sometimes permanently.

## What counts as a service-area business

Google defines a service-area business (SAB) as one that visits customers at their location but does not serve customers at its own address. [Google's guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038163) explain this distinction. If customers come to you, you are a storefront business. If you go to them, you are a service-area business.

Examples:
- A plumber who works out of their garage but visits customers' homes
- A mobile dog groomer who drives to clients
- An HVAC company with a warehouse but no walk-in counter

If you have a warehouse or shop but no customer-facing entrance, you are still an SAB. Google cares whether customers visit your location, not whether you have one.

## Google Business Profile settings for SABs

When you create or edit your GBP, there is a setting for service area. Here is how to configure it correctly.

### Hide your address

If you are an SAB, you must not show your address on your listing. [Google's guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038163) are explicit: service-area businesses should not list their address publicly.

In GBP manager:
1. Go to **Location details**
2. Under **Address**, enter your real address (Google needs it for verification)
3. Check **I deliver goods and services to my customers at their locations**
4. Under **Service area**, add the cities, regions, or ZIP codes you serve
5. Make sure **Show business address to customers** is not enabled

Your address stays on file with Google but does not appear in Maps or Search.

### List your service areas

You can add up to 20 service areas. Use cities or ZIP codes, not radius circles. Google [deprecated radius-based service areas](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9034571) in 2021.

Be honest. List the places you actually go. If you are based in Rockford and you serve Loves Park, Machesney Park, and Belvidere, list those. Do not list Chicago because you did one job there in 2019.

Google uses your service area list to determine which local searches you are eligible for. If someone searches "plumber near me" from Belvidere and you listed Belvidere, you can show up. If you didn't list it, you won't.

### Choose the right category

Same rule as any business: pick the most specific accurate category. "Plumber" not "Services." The category determines eligibility, not the service area list.

## Landing page strategy for each city

Your GBP gets you into the map pack. Your website gets you into the organic results. For service-area businesses, the most effective strategy is a dedicated landing page for each city you serve.

### Why you need city pages

If you serve 5 cities and you have one services page, you are competing for all 5 cities on one page. Google has to figure out which city to rank you for, and it usually picks your headquarters city.

If you have 5 separate pages, each optimized for one city, each page can rank independently. Your Rockford page ranks for "plumber Rockford." Your Belvidere page ranks for "plumber Belvidere."

### What makes a good city page

Not a template with the city name swapped. Google calls those doorway pages and penalizes them. See my guide on [local landing pages without doorway spam](/local-landing-pages-without-doorway-spam/) for the full breakdown.

A good city page has:
- The city name in the title, H1, and URL
- Real content about your work in that city (specific jobs, neighborhoods, local references)
- A unique FAQ section answering questions relevant to that area
- Your service area map or list
- Reviews from customers in that city
- Internal links to your main service pages

### How many pages to create

One per city you actually serve. If you serve 3 cities, make 3 pages. If you serve 15, make 15. Do not create pages for cities you don't serve. That is spam, and it does not work long-term.

## Citation building for SABs

Citations are mentions of your business name, address, and phone number on other websites. For SABs, citations are especially important because you don't have a storefront to anchor you to a location.

### Where to get citations

| Directory | Cost | Priority |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Google Business Profile | Free | Must have |
| Bing Places | Free | Must have |
| Apple Maps | Free | High |
| Yelp | Free | High |
| Facebook | Free | High |
| Better Business Bureau | Paid | Medium |
| Yellow Pages | Paid | Low |
| Industry-specific directories | Varies | Medium |

### NAP consistency

Your Name, Address, and Phone number must be identical across every listing. Same formatting, same abbreviations, same phone number. If your GBP says "Matera Plumbing" and Yelp says "Matera Plumbing LLC," Google sees two different businesses.

For SABs, most directories will still ask for an address. Use your real address even though it is hidden on GBP. The directories need it for verification. Just make sure it matches everywhere.

## Why fake addresses get you suspended

This is the most common SAB mistake. You don't want to use your home address, so you use a coworking space, a virtual office, a friend's shop, or a PO Box.

Google's policy is clear: the address must be where you actually operate. If Google figures out you are using a virtual office, they suspend the listing. And they are getting better at detecting this. They cross-reference with [Street View](https://www.google.com/streetview/), utility records, and other listings at the same address.

### What happens when you get suspended

- Your listing disappears from Maps and Search
- You lose all your reviews (sometimes permanently)
- You have to re-verify, which can take weeks
- Repeat violations can result in a permanent ban

It is not worth it. Use your real address, hide it, and let your service area settings do the work.

## The honest path

Service-area business SEO is slower than storefront SEO because you don't have the address signal. But it works. The businesses that rank are the ones that:

1. Set up GBP correctly as an SAB
2. Build real city landing pages
3. Maintain consistent citations
4. Collect reviews from customers in each service area
5. Post updates and answer Q&A

## How Google Business Profile handles service-area businesses

Google treats SABs differently from storefront businesses in specific ways that affect how you show up in search.

When you set your profile to a service-area business, Google removes your address from the public-facing listing. Your address stays on file for verification and spam detection, but it does not appear in Maps, Search, or the knowledge panel. This means you cannot rank in the local pack for searches that are anchored to a specific address radius. Instead, Google uses your service area list to determine eligibility.

The local pack for an SAB is driven by two things: your service area list and your proximity to the searcher. If someone in Belvidere searches "plumber near me," Google checks whether Belvidere is in your service area and how close your actual address is to Belvidere. A plumber based in Rockford who lists Belvidere as a service area can show up, but a plumber based in Rockford who does not list Belvidere will not.

This is why listing your real service areas honestly matters so much. Every city you add is a city you become eligible for. Every city you skip is a city where you are invisible.

## Service area page structure that avoids doorway page penalties

Google penalizes doorway pages — thin, near-duplicate pages created solely to rank for location keywords. The penalty is real and recovering from it is painful. See [Google's spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#doorway-pages) for details. Here is the structure I use that has never triggered one.

Each city page must have content that would be useful to a real customer in that city, not just a template with the city name swapped. The structure I follow:

1. **Unique H1 and title tag.** "Plumber in Belvidere, IL — 24/7 Emergency Repairs" not "Plumber in [City] — 24/7 Emergency Repairs."
2. **City-specific intro paragraph.** Two to three sentences about your actual work in that city. Name neighborhoods, landmarks, or common issues you see there. If you cannot write this, you probably do not serve that city enough to justify a page.
3. **Services section.** The services you offer in that city, with detail. Not a copy-paste of your main services page.
4. **Local FAQ.** Three to five questions a customer in that city would actually ask. "Do you cover the Belvidere area near I-90?" "How fast can you get to Belvidere from Rockford?"
5. **Reviews from that city.** If you have Google reviews from customers in Belvidere, reference them. This is the strongest signal that the page is real.
6. **Service area map.** A real map showing your coverage area, not a stock image.
7. **Internal links.** Link to your main service pages and to adjacent city pages. Do not link only to your homepage.

The test is simple: if you removed the city name from the page, would the remaining content still be unique and useful? If yes, you have a real page. If the page collapses into generic boilerplate, it is a doorway page and you should rewrite it.

## A real service-area business SEO strategy

Here is the actual strategy I used for a client — an HVAC company based in Rockford, IL serving six surrounding cities.

**Step 1: GBP setup.** Set as service-area business. Address hidden. Service areas listed: Rockford, Loves Park, Machesney Park, Belvidere, Cherry Valley, Roscoe. Category: HVAC contractor. Photos of the truck, the team, and completed jobs.

**Step 2: City landing pages.** Six pages, one per city. Each had a unique intro written from the owner's experience working in that city. The Belvidere page mentioned a specific neighborhood near I-90 where they had done a furnace replacement that winter. The Loves Park page mentioned a condo complex where they service multiple units. Real details, not templates.

**Step 3: Citations.** NAP standardized across Google, Bing, Apple Maps, Yelp, and Facebook. Same format everywhere. Took one afternoon.

**Step 4: Reviews.** Asked every customer in each service area for a Google review. Over four months, they accumulated 14 reviews, with at least two from each city they served.

**Results:** Within 10 weeks, they were in the local pack for "HVAC repair" in four of the six cities. The two they were not ranking in were the most competitive (Rockford itself) and the farthest (Roscoe). By month six, they were in the pack for five of six. The strategy was not clever or complicated. It was consistent execution of the basics.

## Common mistakes service-area businesses make

Beyond the fake address problem, there are three mistakes I see repeatedly.

**Listing cities you do not serve.** You add Chicago to your service area because you want the traffic. You get one lead, drive two hours, and lose money on the job. Meanwhile, Google sees you listing a city where you have no reviews, no citations, and no real presence. This dilutes your relevance signal for the cities you actually serve.

**Using one services page for all cities.** Your "Services" page mentions all six cities in a single paragraph. Google has to guess which city to rank you for, and it usually picks your headquarters. You lose the other five.

**Ignoring GBP posts and Q&A.** Google gives SABs fewer signals to work with than storefronts. GBP posts and Q&A are two of the few active signals you control. A weekly post about a recent job and answering every question in your Q&A section keeps your profile active and signals relevance. Most SABs set up their profile and never touch it again. That is a missed opportunity.
