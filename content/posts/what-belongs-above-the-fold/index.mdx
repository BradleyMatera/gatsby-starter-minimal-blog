Above the fold on a small business website you need five things: what you do, who it is for, where you work, what it costs, and what to do next. If a visitor sees those five things in the first screen without scrolling, they have enough information to decide whether to keep reading or contact you. If any one is missing, they scroll or they leave.

"Above the fold" comes from newspapers. The stories visible when the paper was folded on a newsstand were the ones that sold the paper. On a website, it means what a visitor sees before they scroll. On a phone, that is roughly 600-800 pixels of vertical space. Not much room. Every element has to earn its place.

Here are the five things that belong there, why each one matters, and what happens when it is missing.

## 1. What you do

This is the headline. One sentence that says what your business does. Not a tagline, not a mission statement, not "Welcome to our website."

**Why it matters:** A visitor who lands on your site from a search result or a referral link has no context. They need to know in the first 2 seconds whether they are in the right place. If they cannot tell what you do, they go back to the search results.

**Good:** "Plumbing repairs and installations in Durand, IL."
**Bad:** "Quality service you can trust since 1987."

The first one tells you exactly what the business does and where. The second one tells you nothing. "Quality service" could be anything. A plumber, a mechanic, a cleaning service, a restaurant.

**How to write it:** Use the formula [what you do] + [where you do it]. "HVAC repair and installation in Rock County, Wisconsin." "Roofing and gutters in Winnebago County." Keep it under 12 words.

## 2. Who it is for

Not every visitor is your customer. A residential plumber does not need commercial property managers calling them. A wedding photographer does not need corporate event inquiries. Telling people who you serve helps the right visitors self-select and the wrong visitors move on.

**Why it matters:** If you try to speak to everyone, you speak to no one. A headline that says "Plumbing repairs for homeowners in Durand, IL" tells a homeowner they are in the right place and tells a commercial property manager they should look elsewhere. That saves everyone time.

**Good:** "Plumbing repairs for homeowners in Durand, IL and surrounding areas."
**Bad:** "Plumbing solutions for all your needs."

**How to write it:** Add who you serve to your headline or subheadline. "for homeowners," "for small businesses," "for renters and landlords." Be specific.

## 3. Where you work

Local search is about geography. If a visitor cannot tell whether you serve their area, they will not contact you. This is especially important if you serve a specific region rather than a whole state.

**Why it matters:** A visitor in Beloit, WI who finds a plumber in Durand, IL needs to know if that plumber drives to Beloit. If the site does not say, the visitor assumes no and leaves. If the site says "serving Durand, Davis, Rockford, and Beloit," the visitor knows they are covered.

**Good:** "Serving Durand, Davis, Rockford, and Beloit areas."
**Bad:** No mention of service area, or "serving the Midwest."

**How to write it:** List the cities or counties you actually serve. If you have a service radius, say so: "Within 30 miles of Durand, IL." Do not be vague. If you only work in one county, say one county.

## 4. What it costs

This is the one most small businesses skip. They do not want to list prices because they want to have a conversation first. I understand the reasoning, but it creates friction. Visitors want to know if they can afford you before they pick up the phone.

**Why it matters:** Price is a qualifier. If a visitor sees that a service call is $89, they know whether that fits their budget. If they see no price, they assume it is expensive and leave. Listing even a starting price or a range filters out people who cannot afford you and encourages people who can.

**Good:** "Service calls start at $89. Free estimates on installations."
**Bad:** "Call for pricing." or no mention of cost at all.

**How to write it:** You do not need to list every price. A starting price, a range, or a "starting at" figure is enough. If you offer free estimates, say so. If you have different tiers, list them. See [my pricing page](/pricing/) for an example of transparent pricing.

## 5. What to do next

The call to action. After a visitor reads what you do, who you serve, where you work, and what it costs, they need to know what to do next. If there is no clear next step, they do nothing. They might bookmark the page. They usually leave.

**Why it matters:** A clear CTA gives the visitor a single, obvious action to take. "Call now," "Request a quote," "Book online." One action, not three. Multiple CTAs compete with each other and reduce the chance of any action being taken.

**Good:** A button that says "Call (608) 313-5373" or "Request a Free Estimate."
**Bad:** Three buttons: "Learn More," "About Us," "Contact." No clear primary action.

**How to write it:** Pick one primary action and make it a button. Use action text, not generic text. "Call Now" is better than "Click Here." "Get a Free Estimate" is better than "Submit." Make the button a contrasting color so it stands out.

## Mobile considerations

Above the fold on mobile is roughly 600-800 pixels. Less than half of a desktop screen.

**What to do:**
- Prioritize the headline and the CTA. Those two must be visible.
- The service area and pricing can go in a smaller subheadline below the main headline.
- Use a single column layout. Do not try side-by-side elements on mobile.
- Test on an actual phone. A browser at 375px width is not the same as a real device.

## Good vs bad above-the-fold layout

**Bad layout:**
- Full-width slider with 5 rotating images
- "Welcome to Smith Plumbing" headline
- Three buttons: Home, Services, About
- No phone number, service area, or pricing visible

**Good layout:**
- One static hero image (compressed, under 200KB)
- Headline: "Plumbing Repairs for Homeowners in Durand, IL"
- Subheadline: "Same-day service available. Service calls start at $89."
- One button: "Call (608) 313-5373"
- Phone number visible in header on every page

The good layout tells a visitor everything they need to know in one screen and gives them one clear action to take.

## Bottom line

Above the fold is not about fitting as much as possible into one screen. It is about fitting the right five things: what you do, who it is for, where you work, what it costs, and what to do next. Get those five right and the rest of your page can provide detail. Get them wrong and no one scrolls down to see the rest.
